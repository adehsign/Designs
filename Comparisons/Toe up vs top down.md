This is of course a matter of preference, and I would recommend to try both. The main differences are - 
##### Bind off vs cast on
The top of the sock is the cast on for a top down and bind off for toes up. You want the top of the sock to be stretchy enough to go over the foot, so you can put the sock on comfortably, but also tight enough to stay up and not flare out. 
Personally,  I have no problem casting on with German twisted cast on, and regularly hear the same from people about long tail cast on too (I haven't tried). It is much easier to knit a stretchy cast on than a bind off, and so if you struggle finding that balance, knitting top down generally solves it. 
For the bind off, it does depend on how tightly you bind off (and the depth of you heel) (and the stitch before you bind off), but the surprisingly stretchy bind off usually does a good job. If you bind off very tightly, go for something more stretchy. If you bind off very loosely, or want the sock to flare out less, go for something less stretchy and more structured.
It is hard to say how stretchy a bind off or a cast on will be for you, as it does depend on personal tension, but here are some variants.

##### Toe construction, comparative for directions
Usually, if you knit top down, you will need to seam the edge of the sock. If you knit toe up, you will need to start with very little stitches, with a Turkish cast on or something similar, and it can be a little finicky. Especially for some toe constructions it can be complicated. 

##### Yarn management
If you are not sure you have enough yarn for the size and height of your desired sock, or want to use every last bit of yarn, it is much easier to do with toe up (especially when knitting both socks at the same time (link)). You can divide you yarn in half and just knit up until you almost run out of yarn. Frogging the cuff and knitting it a little lower or higher is simple.
On the other hand, if you knit the sock top down, you need to decide the length of the sock when you start the heel, which is quite early. If you run out of yarn in the end, or have much left, you can't just knit the toes at a different point as it changes the length of the foot and so it changes the fit.

##### Heel flap and gusset and other oriented designs
If you knit a heel flap and gusset top down you will need to pick up stitches, which some people aim to avoid. Lace also has a direction, and so knitting up vs down will give you a flipped design for you lace. This depends on the specific pattern parts you knit and the specific design. 
##### creative solutions for construction direction
You can always choose both and seam - knit the sock toe up, reach the start cuff and stop. Cast on the top of the sock, knit down the cuff and after finishing the cuff, seam them together. This is what I do for vanilla socks that I want to knit toe up, as it is very easy to seam seamlessly.
