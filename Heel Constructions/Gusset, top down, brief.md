when you reach the place where you want to start the gusset, split your stitches in half - top and bottom of your sock. The heel flap and the heel turn will only be knitted on the bottom half.

The heel flap - there are many types of heel flaps you can knit.  The general idea is to use a stronger and denser stitch for a part that experience a lot of pressure. We make a stronger fabric by slipping a lot of stitches. The classic way is to slip 1 with yarn in back, k1 in the right side of the fabric, and purl the wrogn side (but slip the first stitch).
You can also choose a different pattern. If you have an odd number of stitches on the botton, mk1 on the first  row of the heel flap.
make sure to always slip the first stitch and knit of purl (or whatever in pattern) the last one, to make it easier to pick up. 
how deep is the flap? it is of course personal, best way to know it trial and error. you can measure how many stitches do you want around the foot after the flap.
